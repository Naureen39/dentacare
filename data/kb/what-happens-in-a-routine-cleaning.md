title: What happens in a routine cleaning
category: procedures
updated_at: 2026-09-15
---
A routine cleaning removes the plaque and hardened tartar that brushing and flossing cannot reach. A hygienist first checks your gums and measures the small spaces between the gums and teeth. They then use a small scaling tool to remove deposits, and polish your teeth with a gritty paste.

After flossing between the teeth, you may be offered a fluoride treatment, which strengthens the enamel. The dentist checks your teeth, looks for cavities and gum disease and examines your mouth, tongue and throat for early signs of problems.

A cleaning is usually painless, though sensitive gums may feel tender for a day. Most people have one every six months, and people with gum disease or a high risk of decay may benefit from visiting more often.

## Short answer
A cleaning removes plaque and tartar, polishes your teeth and is followed by a dentist exam, often with a fluoride treatment. Most people should have one every six months.
