Tooth sensitivity is a short, sharp twinge when you eat or drink something hot, cold, sweet or acidic, or when you breathe cold air. It often happens when the protective enamel has worn thin or the gums have receded, exposing the softer layer beneath.

Common causes include brushing too hard, grinding your teeth, acidic food and drink, gum recession and tooth decay. A cracked tooth or a loose filling can also cause it. Because several causes need different treatment, it is worth having it checked.

Things that often help are a soft toothbrush, a gentle brushing technique and a toothpaste made for sensitive teeth, used daily. Your dentist may also apply a fluoride varnish or recommend a nightguard if you grind. Book a visit if sensitivity is severe or lasts more than a couple of weeks.

## Short answer
Sensitivity is often caused by worn enamel or receding gums. A soft brush and a sensitive toothpaste help, and you should see us if it is severe or lasts longer than a couple of weeks.
