An extraction removes a tooth that cannot be saved, for example because of severe decay, a fracture, advanced gum disease or a wisdom tooth that is impacted or causing crowding. Your dentist always considers other options first.

After the area is thoroughly numbed, the dentist loosens the tooth and removes it. You should feel pressure but not pain. A surgical extraction, used when the tooth is broken off or buried, involves a small opening in the gum and sometimes dividing the tooth into pieces.

Stitches that dissolve may be placed. You will receive clear written aftercare instructions, and most people feel back to normal within a few days. We discuss replacing the tooth, because gaps can allow neighbouring teeth to drift, and bite problems can follow.

## Short answer
An extraction removes a tooth that cannot be saved, under thorough numbing. Surgical extractions are used for broken or impacted teeth, and replacement options are discussed at the same visit.
