A deep cleaning, called scaling and root planing, treats gum disease by cleaning below the gum line. When bacteria build up under the gums, pockets form around the teeth and the gums become red, swollen or bleed. A regular cleaning cannot reach into those pockets.

The treatment is carried out with numbing, one section of the mouth at a time. The hygienist removes plaque and tartar from the tooth surfaces and the roots, and smooths the roots so that the gum can reattach. Your gums may be sore for a few days, and warm salt water rinses help.

A follow-up visit a few weeks later checks how well the gums have healed. After a deep cleaning, many people move to cleanings every three or four months to keep the gum disease under control.

## Short answer
A deep cleaning removes bacteria and tartar below the gum line with numbing, one section of the mouth at a time. It treats gum disease and is usually followed by more frequent cleanings.
