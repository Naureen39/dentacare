Inside every tooth is soft tissue called the pulp. When it becomes infected or inflamed, from deep decay, a crack or an injury, it can cause severe pain, sensitivity to heat or swelling. Root canal therapy removes the damaged pulp so that the tooth can be saved.

Modern root canal treatment is done with numbing and is usually no more uncomfortable than having a filling. The dentist opens the tooth, cleans and shapes the canals, disinfects them and seals them. A crown is normally placed afterward because a treated tooth can become brittle.

Most people return to normal activity the next day. Saving a natural tooth keeps your bite and jaw stable and avoids the cost of replacing it. Early treatment, as soon as pain begins, gives the best result.

## Short answer
Root canal therapy removes an infected nerve and tissue from inside a tooth so the tooth can be kept. It is done with numbing, and the tooth usually needs a crown afterward.
