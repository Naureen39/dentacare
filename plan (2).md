# Meridian Dental Care: End to End Build Plan

Clinic website, online booking, patient portal, staff console, revenue and analytics dashboard, and a support and booking chatbot.

## 0. Rules for the Developer

1. Use a professional tone everywhere: UI copy, code comments, commit messages, docs, and seed data. No slang, jokes, or placeholder text such as "lorem ipsum" or "test test".
2. Never mention any AI assistant or vendor brand names in code, comments, commits, UI, or docs. The chatbot is named "Meridian Assistant". Model names appear only in environment variables and the internal config.
3. Never use the long dash character anywhere in the repo, UI copy, or docs. Use a hyphen, colon, or comma.
4. Use only free and open source tooling. Every paid service is out of scope.
5. Clinic name, logo, colors, address and phone are configuration, not hardcoded. The clinic is fictional. Show a small "Demo environment, fictional clinic" note in the footer.
6. Never copy copyrighted text or images. Sources for content and images are listed in Section 4.

## 4. Dataset and Content Sources

### 4.1 Primary: synthetic clinic dataset (generated by `scripts/generate_data.py`)

Real patient data is not public, so generate a realistic, reproducible dataset with a fixed random seed. The script must produce 24 months of history ending on the day before the run date.

| Item | Target |
|---|---|
| Patients | about 4,500 (names via Faker, fictional emails on `example.com`) |
| Dentists | 7 (general, orthodontics, pediatric, endodontics, periodontics, oral surgery, cosmetic) |
| Services | 14 (catalog in 4.3) |
| Appointments | 18,000 to 25,000 |
| Invoices and payments | one invoice per completed appointment, with payments |

Behavior to simulate so charts look real:

- Growth: 6 to 10 percent year over year volume growth, with a price increase of 4 percent starting in month 13.
- Seasonality: strong Q4 (insurance benefit expiry), slower July and August, December holiday dip in the last week.
- Weekly pattern: busiest Tuesday to Thursday, light Friday afternoon, Saturday half day, closed Sunday.
- Hours: 8:00 to 18:00 weekdays, 9:00 to 14:00 Saturday, 12:30 to 13:30 lunch block.
- Status mix: about 82 percent completed, 7 to 9 percent cancelled, 8 to 11 percent no-show.
- No-show probability drivers: longer lead time, young adult age band, prior no-shows, no reminder confirmation, early morning and late Friday slots. Calibrate with the reference dataset in 4.2.
- A new dentist starts in month 10 with a ramping schedule. One dentist reduces days in month 18.
- Payer mix: 58 percent insurance, 34 percent self pay, 8 percent payment plan. Insurance payments arrive 14 to 45 days after service. Self pay is collected same day. This creates realistic accounts receivable aging.
- Service mix: cleanings and exams about 45 percent of visits, fillings 18, crowns 8, whitening 6, orthodontic consults 6, root canals 4, extractions 4, implants 3, emergency visits 4, other 2.
- New patient share: 14 to 18 percent of monthly visits, with a retention pattern (returning every 6 months for 60 percent of patients).

Outputs: write directly to PostgreSQL through `scripts/seed.py`, plus CSV exports in `data/generated/` for review. Idempotent: running it twice must not duplicate data (use a `--reset` flag).

### 4.2 Reference dataset (calibration and no-show model validation only)

- Kaggle "Medical Appointment No Shows": `https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/joniarroba/noshowappointments` (about 110,000 rows).
- Download: `pip install kaggle`, place the Kaggle token at `~/.kaggle/kaggle.json`, then `kaggle datasets download -d joniarroba/noshowappointments -p data/reference --unzip`. If the CLI is not set up, download manually from the page and place the CSV in `data/reference/`.
- Use it to: (a) compute no-show rate by lead time bucket, age band, and reminder flag, then set the generator probabilities; (b) validate that the feature pipeline and LightGBM model in Phase 10 behave sensibly (expected AUC roughly 0.70 to 0.75).
- Do not insert its rows into the clinic database. It is not dental data.
- Published dental no-show studies report typical rates in the 5 to 12 percent range, which supports the generator target.

### Phase 2: Database Schema and Migrations

**Critical constraints and indexes**
- Double booking guard: `EXCLUDE USING gist (dentist_id WITH =, slot WITH &&) WHERE (status IN ('booked','confirmed','checked_in','completed'))`.
- Also prevent a patient from overlapping appointments with the same exclusion pattern on `patient_id`.
- Indexes: `appointments (dentist_id, lower(slot))`, `appointments (patient_id)`, `payments (paid_at)`, `invoices (issued_at)`, GIN trigram on patient names.
- Vector indexes: `CREATE INDEX ON kb_chunks USING hnsw (embedding vector_cosine_ops);` and the same for `intent_examples`.
- Row level security is optional. Enforce patient scoping in the service layer and cover it with tests.

**Exit criteria**: `alembic upgrade head` and `downgrade base` both work. A test proves the exclusion constraint rejects overlapping bookings.

### Phase 3: Authentication, Authorization and Security Baseline

**Authentication**
1. Registration (patients only) with email verification link (signed, 24 hour expiry). Password rules: minimum 12 characters, checked against a small common password list.
2. Hashing: argon2id with tuned parameters.
3. Login returns a short lived access JWT (15 minutes, held in memory only on the frontend) and sets a refresh token cookie: `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Strict; Path=/api/v1/auth`.
4. Refresh token rotation with reuse detection: if a revoked token is presented, revoke the whole `family_id` and force login.
5. Lockout: 5 failed attempts triggers a 15 minute lock with a generic error message. Same response time for unknown emails.
6. Password reset by emailed one time token (hashed in DB, 30 minute expiry).
7. TOTP MFA (pyotp) mandatory for admin and dentist roles, optional for others. Provide 8 single use recovery codes (hashed).
8. Endpoints: `/auth/register, /auth/verify-email, /auth/login, /auth/mfa/verify, /auth/refresh, /auth/logout, /auth/forgot, /auth/reset, /auth/me`.

**Authorization**
- Role matrix enforced by FastAPI dependencies `require_roles(...)`.

| Resource | patient | receptionist | dentist | admin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Own profile and appointments | RW | - | - | R |
| All patients | - | RW | R (own patients) | RW |
| Appointments | own | all | own schedule | all |
| Billing | own invoices R | RW | R | RW |
| Analytics dashboard | - | limited (operations only) | own performance | full |
| Services, staff, KB, settings | - | - | - | RW |
| Audit logs | - | - | - | R |

- Object level checks on every endpoint (a patient can never read another patient's record). Write negative tests for each.

**Data protection**
1. Field level encryption (AES-GCM via `cryptography`) for DOB, phone, address, insurance member id. Key from env, with a key id prefix to support rotation.
2. TLS everywhere in production through Caddy. HSTS, `Content-Security-Policy` (no inline scripts), `X-Content-Type-Options`, `Referrer-Policy`, `Permissions-Policy`.
3. CORS allowlist from env. CSRF protection for the refresh and logout endpoints (double submit token or custom header check combined with SameSite).
4. Rate limiting with Redis: login 5 per minute per IP and per account, public booking 20 per hour per IP, chat 30 per minute per session, contact form 5 per hour.
5. Audit log entries for: login, failed login, password change, record view by staff, record change, export, role change. Never log PII values or tokens.
6. Input validation with Pydantic on all inputs, parameterized queries only, upload type and size checks if uploads are added, output encoding by React.
7. Data retention: configurable purge of chat messages older than 90 days, anonymize cancelled guest records after 12 months.
8. Dependency and secret scanning in CI.

**Exit criteria**: complete auth test suite including token reuse, lockout, role denial and cross patient access denial. OWASP ASVS level 1 checklist reviewed and recorded in `docs/security.md`.

### Phase 4: Core Booking Backend

**Availability engine (`services/availability.py`)**
1. Input: `service_id, date range, optional dentist_id`.
2. For each eligible dentist and day: take the weekly template, subtract breaks, schedule exceptions and existing active appointments, then cut free windows into slots on a 15 minute grid that fit the service duration plus a configurable buffer (default 10 minutes cleanup).
3. Convert between clinic timezone and UTC correctly, including daylight saving transitions. Add tests for the transition dates.
4. Respect rules from `app_settings`: minimum notice (2 hours), maximum horizon (90 days), same day booking toggle.
5. Slot hold: when a user selects a slot, create a Redis key `hold:{dentist}:{start}` with 5 minute TTL (SET NX). Booking must present the hold token. The database exclusion constraint is the final guard, and a violation returns HTTP 409 with a clear message and alternative slots.
6. Return shape: `[{date, slots:[{start, end, dentist_id, dentist_name}]}]`, cached in Redis for 30 seconds per query key, invalidated on booking changes.

**Endpoints**
- Public: `GET /public/services`, `GET /public/dentists`, `GET /public/availability`, `POST /public/hold`, `POST /public/appointments` (guest booking: name, email, phone, service, slot, consent; requires email OTP verification, 6 digits, hashed, 10 minute expiry, 5 attempts max), `POST /public/contact`, `POST /public/newsletter`, `GET /public/testimonials`.
- Patient: `GET/PATCH /me`, `GET /me/appointments`, `POST /me/appointments`, `POST /me/appointments/{id}/reschedule`, `POST /me/appointments/{id}/cancel` (policy: free until 24 hours before, then flagged late cancel), `GET /me/invoices`.
- Staff: `GET /staff/schedule` (day, week by dentist), `POST /staff/appointments`, `PATCH /staff/appointments/{id}/status` (checked_in, completed, no_show, cancelled), patient search with trigram, patient create and edit.
- Every status change writes `appointment_status_history` and an audit entry.
- Appointment state machine: booked -> confirmed -> checked_in -> completed. Booked or confirmed -> cancelled. Booked, confirmed -> no_show (after the start time). Reject invalid transitions with 422.

**Exit criteria**: concurrency test with 20 parallel booking attempts on one slot results in exactly one success. Timezone tests pass.

### Phase 7: Knowledge Base and Embeddings

1. `scripts/embed_kb.py` reads `data/kb/*.md`, validates front matter, upserts `kb_documents` by `content_hash`, chunks body text (target 180 tokens, 30 token overlap, keep headings), embeds with fastembed `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`, writes `kb_chunks`. Only changed documents are re-embedded.
2. Normalize vectors and use cosine distance (`<=>`). For queries, prepend the BGE query instruction: `Represent this sentence for searching relevant passages: `. Do not prepend it to documents.
3. Load the embedding model once at API startup and keep it in memory. Run encoding in a thread pool so the event loop is not blocked. Cache query embeddings in Redis by normalized text hash for 24 hours.
4. Retrieval function `search_kb(query, k=3)` returns `(chunk, score, document_id)` ordered by similarity, with an optional category filter.
5. Intent routing data: seed `intent_examples` with 15 to 25 short utterances per intent (greeting, hours, location, pricing, insurance, book, reschedule, cancel, faq_procedure, emergency, human_handoff, thanks, out_of_scope), embedded once.
6. Admin endpoints: list, create, edit KB documents and trigger re-embedding.
7. Evaluate retrieval: write 60 test questions with the expected document id. Target top 3 hit rate of at least 90 percent. Keep this as a pytest quality gate.

**Exit criteria**: retrieval evaluation passes and median query embedding plus vector search time is under 80 ms on CPU.

### Phase 8: LLM Gateway (Groq primary, Gemini fallback)

Build one module `chat/llm_gateway.py` that is the only place any LLM is called.

**Providers**
- Groq: `openai` SDK with `base_url=https://api.groq.com/openai/v1`, model from env. Free tier limits per the Groq rate limit docs at plan time for this model: 30 requests per minute, 1,000 requests per day, 8,000 tokens per minute, 200,000 tokens per day. Re-check the limits page in the Groq console before launch because limits change and apply per organization.
- Gemini: `google-genai` SDK, model from env. Free tier limits vary by model and change often. Read them from Google AI Studio and store them in `app_settings` (`gemini_rpm`, `gemini_rpd`, `gemini_tpm`) rather than hardcoding.
- Both exposed behind one interface: `complete(messages, max_tokens, json_schema=None, purpose)`.

**Routing and budgets**
1. Order: primary from `LLM_PRIMARY`. Fail over to the other when any of these hold: HTTP 429, 5xx, timeout over 8 seconds, or the local budget counter for the primary is above 80 percent of RPM, TPM, or daily limits.
2. Local budget counters in Redis (sliding minute window and per day key, day boundary in each provider's reset timezone). Also read Groq `x-ratelimit-remaining-tokens` and `retry-after` headers to correct the counters.
3. Circuit breaker: after 3 consecutive failures, mark a provider unhealthy for 60 seconds with half open probing.
4. If both providers are unavailable, return a graceful degraded response: top FAQ short answer if available, otherwise a message offering the booking form and the clinic phone number. The chat must never show a raw error.
5. Log every call in `llm_usage` (provider, tokens, latency, purpose). Expose provider and token charts in the admin analytics (Phase 15).

**Token discipline settings**
- `temperature=0.2`, strict `max_tokens` per purpose (see Phase 9 table).
- For `openai/gpt-oss-20b` set low reasoning effort (`reasoning_effort="low"`) and exclude reasoning output, because reasoning tokens count toward limits. Confirm parameter names in the Groq docs for the model.
- Put the static system prompt first and keep it byte identical across calls so Groq prompt caching applies (cached tokens do not count toward rate limits). Put dynamic content after it.
- Structured output: Gemini with `response_mime_type=application/json` and `response_schema`, Groq with JSON mode or a strict schema prompt. Validate with Pydantic and do one repair retry at most.
- Do not use model tool calling or agent loops. All actions are executed by backend code.

**Exit criteria**: tests with mocked providers prove failover on 429, timeouts, budget thresholds, and circuit breaker recovery. A manual test with real keys passes for both providers.

### Phase 9: Chatbot Orchestration (minimum token design)

**Principle**: the database and code do the work, the LLM is the last resort. Most turns must cost zero tokens.

**Turn pipeline** (`chat/router.py`)
1. **Safety pre checks (0 tokens)**: length cap 500 characters, strip control characters, emergency keyword rules (severe swelling, trouble breathing, uncontrolled bleeding, facial trauma). Emergency match returns a fixed message with the emergency number and clinic emergency line.
2. **PII handling**: mask card numbers and government ids by regex before storing or sending anywhere.
3. **Active flow check**: if the session `state` has an active booking, reschedule or cancel flow, route to the state machine (step 5).
4. **Intent detection (0 tokens)**: embed the message (cached), compare with `intent_examples` by cosine similarity. Similarity of 0.80 or higher picks the intent. Between 0.65 and 0.80 with FAQ like text goes to RAG. Below 0.65 goes to the fallback.
5. **Booking state machine (0 to 1 call)**: slots to fill: service, preferred dentist (optional), date, time, contact details (or logged in identity), consent, OTP for guests.
   - The UI renders quick reply chips and a date and time slot picker for each step, so most input is clicks, not text.
   - Availability always comes from the availability service, never from the LLM.
   - If the user types free text with several details ("cleaning next Tuesday morning with Dr Patel"), make one extraction call: Gemini or Groq returns JSON `{service, dentist, date_expr, time_pref}` and code resolves dates with `dateparser` against clinic timezone. Try a rule based parser first and call the LLM only if confidence is low.
   - Final confirmation, success, and error messages use fixed templates.
   - Reschedule and cancel require authentication or a signed link plus OTP.
6. **FAQ and policy (0 or 1 call)**: run `search_kb(query, k=3)`.
   - Top score 0.82 or higher: return the document `short_answer` with a "Read more" link. 0 tokens.
   - Score 0.65 to 0.82: one LLM call with the top 2 chunks (maximum 350 context tokens) and the question.
   - Below 0.65: say it cannot answer, offer the contact form, phone, or a human callback request (stored as a `contact_inquiries` row).
7. **Greeting, thanks, hours, location, simple pricing**: templates filled from the database (0 tokens). Pricing answers read from `services`.
8. **Response cache**: Redis key by normalized question hash for FAQ LLM answers, 24 hour TTL.

**Context management**
- Send at most the last 4 turns plus a rolling summary (maximum 80 tokens), regenerated only when the session exceeds 8 turns and only by rule based truncation of old turns (no extra LLM call).
- System prompt maximum 250 tokens: role, clinic scope, tone, refusal rules, "answer only from provided context", maximum 3 sentences.
- Never include full documents. Only top chunks.

**Token budget table (enforced as constants and tested)**

| Purpose | Max prompt tokens | Max completion tokens | LLM calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entity extraction (booking free text) | 350 | 80 | 0 or 1 |
| FAQ answer with context | 700 | 160 | 0 or 1 |
| Fallback rephrase | 300 | 100 | rare |
| Complete booking flow | 0 | 0 | 0 to 1 total |

Target: average under 1,200 total tokens per completed conversation and over 70 percent of turns resolved with zero LLM calls. Track both in analytics.

**Guardrails**
- User text is always placed in a delimited user message and never concatenated into the system prompt. No tool access, no database access by the model. Outputs are validated (JSON schema, max length, link allowlist).
- No diagnosis or medication advice. Include a short fixed disclaimer on clinical questions and route to booking.
- Anonymous users can chat and start a guest booking. Account data (own appointments) requires login.
- Handoff: "Talk to a person" creates an inquiry and shows clinic phone and hours.

**Endpoints**: `POST /chat/sessions`, `POST /chat/sessions/{id}/messages` (supports server sent events for streaming text), `POST /chat/sessions/{id}/feedback` (thumbs up or down), `GET /chat/sessions/{id}`.

**Exit criteria**: scripted conversation tests for 25 scenarios (FAQ, booking, reschedule, cancel, emergency, out of scope, injection attempts, provider failure) pass. Measured zero LLM call share is at least 70 percent in the test set.

### Phase 10: Analytics Backend, Forecasting and No-Show Model

**Metric definitions** (document in `docs/metrics.md`, one source of truth)
- Gross billed revenue: sum of invoice totals by `issued_at`.
- Collected revenue: sum of payments by `paid_at`.
- Collection rate: collected divided by billed for the same cohort period.
- Utilization: booked minutes divided by available schedule minutes.
- No-show rate: no_show divided by (completed + no_show). Cancellation rate: cancelled divided by all scheduled.
- Average revenue per visit, new patients, returning patients, 6 month retention.
- Lead time: days between booking creation and appointment date.
- Net new patients, patient lifetime value (cumulative revenue per patient).

**Implementation**
1. SQL views and materialized views: `mv_daily_revenue`, `mv_monthly_revenue`, `mv_service_mix`, `mv_dentist_performance`, `mv_utilization_daily`, `mv_hourly_heatmap`, `mv_payer_mix`, `mv_ar_aging`, `mv_cohort_retention`, `mv_chatbot_daily`. Refresh nightly and on demand for admin (`REFRESH MATERIALIZED VIEW CONCURRENTLY`).
2. Endpoints under `/analytics` (admin full, receptionist operations only, dentist own data), all accepting `from, to, granularity (day, week, month, quarter), dentist_id, service_id, payer_type`:
   - `/summary` (KPI cards with value, previous period value, percent change, sparkline series)
   - `/revenue/trend` (billed and collected, with prior year series)
   - `/revenue/by-service`, `/revenue/by-dentist`, `/revenue/by-payer`
   - `/appointments/status-trend`, `/appointments/heatmap` (weekday by hour), `/appointments/lead-time`
   - `/patients/new-vs-returning`, `/patients/retention-cohorts`
   - `/finance/ar-aging`, `/finance/collection-rate`
   - `/forecast/revenue` (next 3 months with 80 percent interval)
   - `/no-show/upcoming-risk` (next 7 days list with score and top drivers)
   - `/chatbot/summary` (conversations, resolution without human, zero LLM share, booking conversion, tokens by provider, failover count, feedback score)
   - `/export/csv` for any table view.
3. Forecast: statsmodels Holt-Winters (additive trend, seasonal period 12) on monthly collected revenue, fallback to seasonal naive when history is shorter than 24 months. Return point forecast and interval.
4. No-show model (`scripts/train_no_show.py`): LightGBM on leakage safe features known at booking time: lead time, weekday, hour, age band, new or returning, previous no-show count and rate, service category, reminder confirmed flag, payer type, previous late cancels. Time based train and test split (train on the first 18 months, test on the last 6). Report AUC, precision at top decile, and calibration. Save model with `joblib`, record version and metrics in a `model_registry` row. Explain scores with LightGBM feature contributions (`pred_contrib`) for the top 3 drivers per appointment.
5. Cache analytics responses in Redis for 5 minutes keyed by filters.

**Exit criteria**: every endpoint has tests against the seeded dataset with known expected numbers. Reconciliation test: sum of monthly revenue equals sum of payments in the database.
